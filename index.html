Peer benchmarking

Is this borrower an outlier against companies in the same industry?
How to read this view
  1. The Peer Group Anomaly Index places the borrower on its industry's distribution for each metric. The marker shows the percentile; the shaded ends mark the bottom and top 10%, where a value is unusual and needs an explanation.
  2. An outlier is not bad by itself. It is a prompt to ask management why this company differs from its peers.
  3. The comparison table lists every peer in the industry. Click a row to switch borrower.

Credit summary

Overall view of the borrower: grade, the drivers behind it and the action it suggests.
How to read this view
  1. The grade box shows the credit watch grade for the selected year. The five pillar bars show where the risk comes from (longer bar means more risk).

Borrower assessment

Liquidity

Can the borrower meet obligations falling due in the next twelve months, and is its liquidity real?
How to read this view
  1. The signal table starts with the traditional current ratio and moves to measures that test whether liquidity is real: operating cash flow ratio, cash conversion efficiency, time-adjusted current ratio and defensive interval.
  2. A healthy current ratio with red signals further down is the classic pattern of a borrower whose current assets are slow to turn into cash.
  3. The realisation weights for the time-adjusted current ratio can be changed with the sliders to suit the industry.
Borrower assessment

Solvency and debt capacity

Can the borrower service its debt, and how much more can it safely carry?
How to read this view
  1. The top row shows leverage and coverage for the selected year with their signals.
  2. The debt capacity panel sizes the maximum debt the borrower's cash flow can support at a target DSCR. Move the sliders to test a loan proposal. Headroom is the gap between that maximum and existing borrowings.
  3. The debt structuring matrix places the borrower by asset strength and cash strength and shows the approach that fits.
Borrower assessment

Distress models

Do the established bankruptcy models agree that the borrower is at risk of default?
How to read this view
  1. Five models are run side by side. Each uses a different mix of liquidity, profitability, leverage and cash flow, so agreement between them carries more weight than any single score.
  2. The Altman version is chosen by industry: Z' (book equity, with asset turnover) for manufacturers and Z'' (without asset turnover) for service and other firms. The market-value Z is shown for the latest year.
  3. The Ohlson panel breaks the score into its components, so the driver of the probability is visible.
Borrower assessment

Earnings quality

Are the profits that will repay the loan backed by cash, and are there signs of manipulation?
How to read this view
  1. Accrual measures come first: the higher the share of profit that is not cash, the less a lender should rely on it.
  2. The Beneish M-score, Montier C-score and Piotroski F-score follow, with every component shown. Accrual and real earnings management are measured against the cross-section of Indian listed firms.
  3. Benford's law tests the digit pattern of every reported number. It does not prove manipulation; it indicates where to look.
Borrower assessment

Red flags and governance

Do the statements show any of the patterns seen in known Indian fraud and default cases?
How to read this view
  1. Each check is a rule applied to the reported numbers. The evidence column shows the numbers behind the result, and the last column explains why a lender should care.
  2. Case checks are drawn from SEBI and forensic cases: revenue inflation through subsidiaries, loan-to-equity conversion, advances to related parties and quarter-end revenue acceleration.
  3. Behavioural checks compare the borrower with its own history rather than with a fixed threshold.
Lending decision

Borrower quality rating

What is the borrower's final rating once the analyst's judgement is added to the numbers?
How to read this view
  1. The rating combines the quantitative score from tabs 4 to 8 (60%) with a qualitative scorecard (40%) and maps the result to a 1 to 10 scale, where 1 is the best borrower.
  2. Six qualitative factors are pre-filled from the data, with the evidence shown. Management, board and auditor cannot be read from the statements; assess them from the annual report and select a level.
  3. A notch override of up to two grades is allowed for information outside the scorecard, with a recorded reason. Choices are saved in this browser.
Lending decision

Loan structuring and covenants

How much can be lent, on what security, and under which covenants?
How to read this view
  1. Choose the loan type and the proposed amount. The maximum loan is the lowest of four limits: the lender's leverage limit for the rating, the gearing limit, the DSCR-based debt capacity and, for secured loans, the security cover.
  2. The lender's policy (limits by rating band, extra leverage allowed for secured lending, required cover and loss given default) can be edited to match the institution.
  3. Financial covenants are tested on pro-forma figures after the loan. Non-financial covenants, such as prior consent for further borrowing, are recommended from the borrower's own red flags. Download the credit memo for the file.

Import statements

Assess a borrower that is not in the Nifty 500, or cross-check figures with Prowess or Bloomberg data.

1. Prepare the file

Any source works: Prowess dx, Bloomberg, Capitaline or the annual report.
  1. Export the annual income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement.
  2. Arrange them in one sheet: first column holds the line item, the first row holds the years (oldest to newest).
  3. Save as CSV. Amounts should be in Rs crore; use the unit selector if the source is in Rs million or lakh.
  4. Line names are matched automatically (for example "Revenue from operations", "Finance costs", "Trade receivables"). The template lists every recognised field.

2. Load and analyse

The file is read in the browser and is not uploaded anywhere.

How to use the Credit Risk Monitor

A credit analyst's workstation for the non-financial companies of the Nifty 500. It answers one question in stages: should this borrower be lent to, and on what terms?

  1. Start at the portfolioTab 1 ranks every borrower by Borrower Quality Rating (BQR). Filter by industry or Altman zone, then click a row.
  2. Read the credit summaryTab 3 gives the grade, the five pillar scores, the analyst note and the suggested action.
  3. Test liquidityTab 4 checks whether current assets really turn into cash (CCE, OCF ratio, TACR, defensive interval).
  4. Size the debtTab 5 shows leverage and coverage, and sizes the maximum loan at a target DSCR. Move the sliders to test a proposal.
  5. Run the distress modelsTab 6 runs Altman, Ohlson, Zmijewski, Springate and Fulmer side by side.
  6. Check earnings qualityTab 7 covers accruals, Beneish, Montier, Piotroski, AEM, REM and Benford's law.
  7. Review red flagsTab 8 lists rule-based checks drawn from Indian fraud and default cases, with the evidence.
  8. Rate the borrowerTab 9 combines the numbers (60%) with a qualitative scorecard (40%) into the Borrower Quality Rating, 1 (best) to 10.
  9. Structure the loanTab 10 sets the loan type, sizes the maximum loan under the lender's leverage limits, tests covenants and produces a credit memo.
  10. Compare with peersTab 2 places the borrower within its industry. Tab 11 imports any other company from a CSV file.

Ratings

The Borrower Quality Rating (BQR 1 best to 10 worst) is the final rating: 60% quantitative and 40% qualitative. Grades 1 to 6 are lent to on normal terms, 7 on tighter terms, 8 on a secured basis only, and 9 to 10 receive no fresh lending. The quantitative grade below summarises the numbers alone.
